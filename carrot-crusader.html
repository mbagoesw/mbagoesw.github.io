About

What is Carrot Crusader?

Carrot Crusader is a chaotic 2D arcade shooter where you play as a carrot knight — a helmet-wearing, bow-wielding warrior with one goal: survive the rabbit apocalypse. Waves of aggressive bunnies swarm you from all directions and the only way out is to keep shooting.

The game is built around a kill-streak multiplier system that rewards consistent play. Stay alive, don't get hit, and your score multiplier climbs from ×1 all the way to ×5. Take damage and it resets. Health packs spawn every 30 seconds to give you a fighting chance.

Inspiration

Where It Started

Carrot Crusader started as a learning project following a GDQuest tutorial on building a survivor-style game in Godot. After finishing the tutorial i wasn't satisfied with just having a clone, so i started rebuilding it with my own twist.

The chaotic bunny-vs-carrot theme came from wanting something that felt immediately funny and readable. I looked at Brotato, Vampire Survivors and 20 Minutes Till Dawn for how they handled enemy swarming, progression and score feedback, then tried to distil those ideas into something smaller and more focused.

Development

Challenges I Faced

01
Making enemy spawning feel fair

One of the hardest parts was balancing the enemy spawning system. At first enemies spawned in awkward waves or all at once after delays, which made the pacing feel inconsistent. I had to rework the spawning logic so defeated enemies would immediately be replaced while still keeping the difficulty progression manageable over time.

02
Expanding the game beyond the original prototype

The project started from a simple learning prototype, but once I began adding tutorials, score multipliers, save systems, enemy variants, and UI polish, the codebase became much more complex. Changing systems without breaking existing gameplay became a major challenge throughout development.

03
Designing readable visuals while keeping the style simple

Since the game uses soft pastel colors and simple character designs, it was surprisingly difficult to make enemies stand out clearly from the environment without making them visually overwhelming. I spent a lot of time experimenting with color palettes and enemy variants.

Reflection

What I Learned

01
Small polish details can completely change the feel of a game

Things like hit flashes, score animations, screen feedback, cursor behavior, sound effects and UI transitions added far more to the experience than I originally expected. Even simple mechanics felt more satisfying once they had proper feedback and polish.

02
Going beyond the tutorial

The most valuable part wasn't the tutorial itself but everything I had to figure out after it. Custom enemy behaviour, score systems, UI flow, and save data none of that was in the tutorial.

03
Godot 4 workflow and GDScript

This was my first serious Godot 4 project. I learned how the scene system works, how signals connect components and how GDScript handles things differently from C#.
